How burn windows are counted

An hour is in Rx when temperature, RH, 20-ft wind, gust and (if set) ventilation all meet your limits. A burn day is a day with at least the minimum number of consecutive in-Rx hours inside the burn period.

72-Hour: NWS gridded forecast (api.weather.gov) for the unit's centroid, including mixing height and transport wind for ventilation.

16-Day: ECMWF/GFS blend from Open-Meteo, hourly. 20-ft wind is estimated as 10-m wind ÷ 1.15. Ventilation uses boundary-layer height × 120-m wind as a transport-wind stand-in.

Yearly Availability: ERA5 reanalysis (≈25 km / ERA5-Land ≈9 km for surface fields) via Open-Meteo, hourly, for the selected number of complete years. Shows burn days for every year and month, the typical (mean) month, and the share of burn-period hours in Rx. Reanalysis smooths terrain: compare to your nearest RAWS before relying on absolute counts.

Monthly Outlook (experimental): ECMWF SEAS5 monthly anomalies for the next ~6 months (temperature, dew point, 10-m wind; forecast minus the model's own climatology, so the coarse model's bias cancels). Each anomaly is added to every hour of that calendar month in the ERA5 history at the unit, RH is recomputed from the shifted temperature and dew point, and your full hourly Rx and burn-period rule is re-applied. The outlook is the mean across historical years; the range is the 10th–90th percentile year. Cross-check with CPC and Predictive Services outlooks.
