How the screening estimate works

Emissions = acres × fuel consumed (tons/ac) × PM2.5 emission factor (g/kg) for the fuel preset. Flaming-phase emissions are spread over the burn hours; the preset's smoldering share is held back, and half of it is assumed to be released overnight.

Day: ground-level Gaussian plume for each burn hour using NWS transport wind and mixing height, Pasquill–Turner stability from the 20-ft wind, Briggs open-country dispersion, Briggs plume rise from the fire's heat release (capped at 80% of mixing height), reflection from the ground and mixing-layer top, and fully mixed beyond that. The overlay shows the highest hour at each spot.

Night (PB-Piedmont concept): overnight smoldering emissions carried down the traced drainage by a 1 m/s, 100 m deep drainage flow whose width grows downstream; concentration = emission rate ÷ (speed × depth × width). Fog/superfog and roadway visibility are not modeled — use PB-Piedmont-Web for that.

Colors follow 24-hour PM2.5 AQI breakpoints applied to hourly estimates, so they read conservatively.
